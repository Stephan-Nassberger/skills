# English pattern catalog

This catalog supplements the language-neutral workflow in `SKILL.md`. An
isolated phrase is ordinary language. Repetition and clustering are the useful
signals.

## Content patterns

### Significance inflation

Watch for phrases such as “serves as a testament,” “pivotal moment,”
“underscores its importance,” “reflects broader trends,” “key turning point,”
“evolving landscape,” “indelible mark,” and “setting the stage for.”

Replace inflated interpretation with a supported fact. If the source contains
no supporting detail, shorten the claim instead of inventing one.

### Notability dropping

Claims that something “has been featured in X, Y, and Z,” has an “active social
media presence,” or received “independent coverage” often substitute mentions
for substance. State the relevant fact from a named source or remove the claim.

### Superficial `-ing` tails

Sentence endings built around “highlighting,” “underscoring,” “emphasizing,”
“showcasing,” “reflecting,” “ensuring,” or “fostering” often append an
interpretation that the preceding statement does not establish. Remove it or
write a separate, supported sentence.

### Promotional language

Common signals include “boasts,” “vibrant,” figurative “rich,” “nestled,” “in
the heart of,” “breathtaking,” “stunning,” “renowned,” “must-visit,”
“groundbreaking,” and “commitment to.”

Do not trade these for new imagery. Use an existing detail or omit the
description.

### Vague attribution

“Industry reports,” “observers have cited,” “experts argue,” and “some critics
argue” hide the source of a claim. Name the source, mark the sentence as the
author's assessment, or delete it.

### Challenge and outlook formulas

“Despite these challenges,” “continues to thrive,” “challenges and
opportunities,” and “future outlook” often form a ceremonial objection followed
by generic reassurance. Preserve a real limitation without automatically
resolving it.

## Language patterns

### Vocabulary

This is a moving watchlist, not a ban list:

- delve, tapestry, testament, intricate, meticulous, realm, vibrant
- align with, fostering, showcasing, enhance, crucial, pivotal
- abstract “landscape,” interplay, garner, underscore
- emphasizing, highlighting, ensuring
- repeated use of “concrete” in editorial commentary

Structural repetition is stronger evidence than any word on this list.

### Copula avoidance

“Serves as,” “stands as,” “functions as,” and “represents a” often mean “is.”
“Boasts,” “features,” and “offers” may simply mean “has.”

### Negative parallelism

Rewrite repeated frames such as “not only X but also Y,” “it's not just about
X, it's Y,” and “X rather than Y” when a direct positive statement says the
same thing.

### Filler and hedging

| Before | After |
| --- | --- |
| in order to | to |
| due to the fact that | because |
| at this point in time | now |
| has the ability to | can |
| It is important to note that | remove and state the point |

Keep one meaningful qualifier. Do not stack “could,” “potentially,” and
“possibly.”

### Synonym cycling

If one character becomes “the protagonist,” “the main character,” “the central
figure,” and “the hero” across adjacent sentences, choose the clearest term and
repeat it.

### Chat and markup artifacts

Remove conversational residue such as “I hope this helps,” “Certainly!”,
“Great question!”, “You're absolutely right,” “Would you like me to…”, and “As
of my last training update.” Remove internal citation markers, tool output
labels, unused named references, and tracking parameters in citation URLs.

## Typography and format

- In English, an unspaced em dash—like this—is a common house style. Repeated
  punchy dash pivots are the signal, not the character itself. Match the target
  publication's spacing.
- Match the platform's quotation-mark convention. Curly quotes are correct in
  many edited publications; straight quotes are common in code-oriented and
  wiki contexts.
- Follow the publication's heading case. Do not impose title case everywhere.
- Remove mechanical bold-label lists, decorative emoji, unmotivated tables,
  skipped heading levels, and horizontal rules when the format does not call
  for them.

## Not signals

Correct grammar, formal or academic language, transition words in isolation,
and restrained prose are not evidence of AI writing. Never add mistakes to
make a text appear human.
